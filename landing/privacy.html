Privacy policy

Effective November 22, 2026

This policy explains what happens to your data when you use Chakra — the browser extension for Chrome and Firefox and the Android app — and this website. Chakra is free, open-source software made by Chakra Developers (“we”).

In short

  • We collect no personal data: no account, no sign-up, no analytics, no ads, no tracking, no automatic crash reports.
  • We have no servers that receive your data. Your keys and your recovery phrase never leave your device unencrypted.
  • To work, Chakra talks directly to public blockchain services. Like with any wallet, they see your public addresses and your IP address.

1. What stays on your device

  • Recovery phrases and private keys, encrypted with your password (AES-256-GCM, with a key derived by PBKDF2-SHA256). They leave the device only inside an encrypted backup that you choose to export.
  • Your wallets and accounts: names, settings, the sites you connected, a local copy of your balances and history, optional API keys you enter, and the tabs and site data of the in-app browser.
  • Error reports: when something goes wrong, Chakra keeps a short description of the last errors — the app version, the system version and device model, the screen and the place in the code where it happened. Addresses, keys, amounts and the parameters of web addresses are removed before it is saved.

Uninstalling the app, removing the extension or resetting the wallet deletes all of it. We can't see, recover or delete this data for you, because we never have it.

2. What the services Chakra talks to can see

To show balances, prices and history and to send transactions, Chakra contacts independent services directly from your device. Each of them receives your IP address and what the request needs — usually your public addresses, and for a quote the tokens and amounts. When you confirm a transaction, it receives the signed transaction to publish it. None of them ever receives your keys or your recovery phrase. They are not run by us and have their own privacy policies.

  • Blockchain nodes and indexers: the public nodes of each network (for example PublicNode and the networks’ own endpoints), Blockscout, Routescan, TON Center, TonAPI, TronGrid and mempool.space. The Android app also asks them periodically in the background, to notify you of funds received or sent.
  • Prices and exchange rates: DefiLlama, Frankfurter (European Central Bank rates), CoinGecko, STON.fi and Jupiter.
  • Swaps and bridges, only when you ask for a quote: KyberSwap, Velora, OpenOcean, LI.FI, Jupiter, STON.fi, SUN.io, Relay, Across, deBridge and Symbiosis.
  • Private transaction channels, to protect your swaps from front-running: Flashbots Protect on Ethereum and Jito on Solana.
  • Prediction markets: Polymarket, when you use the Predictions section.
  • Connections with sites on other devices: the WalletConnect relay and the TON Connect bridge. The messages are end-to-end encrypted; the relay sees only that a connection exists, and your IP address.
  • Images and files: token icons (CoinGecko, Trust Wallet’s public assets on GitHub), NFT images (IPFS and Arweave gateways, or the address chosen by each collection), and — only when you compile a contract — the Solidity compiler and libraries (soliditylang.org, jsDelivr, GitHub).
  • Services you add an API key for (optional): Etherscan, Alchemy, Helius, NodeReal, OpenSea, Getgems, TonAPI and UniSat. The key stays on your device and is sent only to its own service.
  • Websites you open in the Android in-app browser see what any browser sends them and, if you connect, your public address. The in-app browser is Android’s WebView, which checks the pages you open with Google Safe Browsing to warn you about phishing and malware; Android manages this check under Google’s privacy policy.

Public blockchains are public: anything you do on them, such as a transfer, can be seen by anyone.

3. Error reports you send

An error report leaves your device only if you send it yourself, after reading its full text in the app (Settings › Report a problem, or the error screen). You choose how:

  • By e-mail to info@chakrawallet.dev, through your own e-mail app: we also receive your e-mail address and anything you write.
  • As an issue on GitHub: it is public, and GitHub’s terms and privacy statement apply.

We use reports and messages only to fix problems and to answer you, and we keep e-mails only as long as that takes.

4. Permissions

The Android app asks for these permissions only when a feature needs them, and you can refuse them:

  • Camera: to scan QR codes. Codes are read on the device; the image is never stored or sent. Sites in the in-app browser can use the camera only after you allow it.
  • Microphone and location: only when a site in the in-app browser asks for them and you allow it; they go to that site.
  • Notifications: for funds received or sent and bridges arrived. They are created on the device.
  • Fingerprint: Android checks it; Chakra never sees your fingerprint, only whether it matched.

The browser extension needs access to the sites you visit so that they can find the wallet and ask it to connect. It doesn’t read or record the content of the pages.

5. This website

chakrawallet.dev is a static site hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies, has no analytics and loads nothing from other sites. GitHub may log visits (such as IP addresses) for security, under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The page that opens TON Connect links reads the name and icon of the site that sent the link from that site.

The stores you download Chakra from (Google Play, Chrome Web Store, Firefox Add-ons) handle downloads and updates under their own privacy policies.

6. Children

Chakra is not meant for children under 16, or under the minimum age set by the law of their country.

7. Your rights

We hold no personal data about you unless you write to us: then we have your e-mail address and your messages, and you can ask us at any time to see them or delete them. For data held by the services listed above, contact them directly.

8. Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new date. If a change concerns what data leaves your device, the app will tell you before it applies.
